How the Near You Feed Works

A transparent look at the algorithm behind the ATlas Near You feeds. No black boxes here.

Some of these factors can be adjusted per-user in your Feed Settings. Look for the User Configurable badge on each factor. More settings will become user-configurable over time.

The Two Feeds

ATlas provides two feeds on Bluesky:

Near You (Chronological)

Every post from people near you, sorted by time. Newest first. No scoring, no ranking, no algorithm deciding what you see first. If someone in your area posted it, it shows up.

Near You FYP (For You)

The same posts, but ranked by a scoring algorithm. Posts that are more relevant to you appear higher. This page explains how that scoring works.

Both feeds share the same data. The only difference is sort order: time vs. score.

What Gets Into the Feed

User Configurable

A post appears in your feed if:

  • ATlas users posts in your city (or nearby cities, up to 250km away), OR
  • The post contains a city hashtag like #pdx, #nyc, or #chicago (if hashtag ingestion is enabled)

While we do include posts from nearby cities, we do not currently pull in hashtags from user posts from nearby cities.

You can control which post sources appear (ATlas users, hashtag posts, or both) and which cities are included (main city, all tagged cities, or exact only) in your Feed Settings.

Since ATlas lets you add up to 3 cities to your account, you can choose to:

  • Only show your main city + nearby cities
  • All your tagged cities + nearby cities
  • Only your tagged cities and not nearby cities
  • A fourth option, only main primary city and not nearby cities, is coming

How Scoring Works (FYP)

Every post starts with a score of 1.0. Then a series of factors multiply that score up or down. The final score determines ranking. Here is every factor:

Engagement

Base Engagement boost

Posts with more likes score higher. A post with 10 likes scores roughly 10x a post with 1 like. Reposts and replies can also contribute but are weighted separately (currently set to zero by default).

Feed Engagement Boost boost

Posts that received likes, reposts, replies, or quotes specifically through our feed get a boost. This rewards content that resonates with the Near You community specifically, not just general Bluesky popularity.

Engagement Velocity boost off by default

When enabled, posts gaining likes quickly get a boost. 20 likes in 1 hour is more interesting than 20 likes over 24 hours. Capped to prevent runaway scores.

Location

Local Boost boost

Posts from your own city get a significant boost over posts from nearby cities. This protects smaller cities from being overwhelmed by content from larger neighbors.

Distance Decay decay

Posts from farther away score lower. A post from 10km away scores much higher than one from 200km away. Beyond the radius cutoff, posts are excluded entirely (or score near-zero if soft cutoff is enabled).

Hashtag Post Weight slight penalty

Posts matched via city hashtags (from non-ATlas users) get a small scoring penalty compared to posts from ATlas-tagged users. This is because hashtag matching has lower location confidence — someone might use #portland while talking about Portland, Maine instead of Oregon.

Freshness

User Configurable

Freshness Decay decay

Older posts score lower. A post from 1 hour ago scores much higher than one from 2 days ago. The decay rate is configurable — you can set your preference to "Fresh only" (aggressive decay), "Today" (moderate), or "Catch up" (gentle, good if you check the feed less often).

Social Signals

User Configurable

Follow Boost boost

Posts from people you mutually follow get a boost. Posts from people you follow (one-way) get a smaller boost. If you're mutuals, only the mutual boost applies (they don't stack). You can adjust the strength of the mutual boost in your feed settings.

Interaction Affinity subtle boost

If you've liked several posts from a particular author in the past, their future posts get a small boost. This scales logarithmically — a few interactions give a noticeable effect, but it plateaus quickly to prevent filter bubbles. The maximum boost is intentionally small.

Follower Normalization boost for small accounts off by default

When enabled, posts are scored partly on how well they perform relative to the author's audience size. A 200-follower account getting 10 likes is more impressive than a 200,000-follower account getting 10 likes. This helps smaller local voices compete with large accounts.

Content Signals

User Configurable

Media Boost off by default

When enabled, posts with images or video get a scoring boost. Currently set to neutral (1.0x). You can filter to media-only or text-only posts in your feed settings.

User Configurable

Alt Text Penalty penalty

Image posts where the author did not add alt text (image descriptions for accessibility) get a small scoring penalty. You can make this stricter in your settings, or set it to hide posts without alt text entirely.

User Configurable

Self Post Weight neutral

Your own posts appear normally by default. You can hide them in your feed settings if you prefer a pure discovery experience.

Interests

User Configurable

Interest boost demotion

A unified interest system that learns what you care about. The system has 160 interests across 9 categories (Creative, Sports, Health & Wellness, Technology, Social & Community, Lifestyle, Academic, Entertainment, NSFW), organized into 67 top-level interests and 93 sub-interests.

How posts get interest-tagged

Posts are matched against 1,475+ term mappings using three methods:

  • Hashtags — exact match against post hashtags (e.g. #cycling, #urbanplanning)
  • Keywords — multi-word phrases detected in post text with word boundary checks via Aho-Corasick (e.g. "bike lane", "climate change", "affordable housing")
  • Domains — link domains in the post (e.g. arxiv.org for Science)

Each mapping has a strength (strong/medium/weak). A strong match tags the post immediately. Medium and weak matches accumulate — two mediums or several weaks together can tag a post. This prevents false positives from generic terms while catching posts that discuss a topic without using specific hashtags.

How interests boost your feed

If you choose interests (e.g. Cycling) at nearyou.atls.city, posts about cycling get an immediate boost. The system also learns from your feed likes over a rolling window — topics you frequently like get a gradual boost, topics you consistently skip get a mild demotion.

Content + Author stacking: Learned signals come from likes on posts tagged with an interest and from likes on posts by authors tagged with that interest. Posts with direct content matches (hashtags, keywords) get the strongest boost. Author-profile matches are weaker. When both content and author match, the boost is stronger than either alone.

Sub-interests: Sub-interests (e.g. NBA under Sports, Hip Hop under Music) earn their own learned boost. Child signals also roll up to parent interests. Parent boost can still apply to child-tagged posts, and multiple matches stack with diminishing returns.

Demotion: Demotion is ratio-based against your baseline like rate and only activates after a minimum number of seen posts for that interest. Interests you explicitly choose are protected from demotion. The exact thresholds are configurable (not hardcoded).

Interest Filter filter

If you have hidden a topic in your feed settings (e.g. hidden Crypto), posts matching that topic via content detection are completely removed from your feed.

Personalization

Liked Post Demotion demotion

Posts you have already liked get pushed down significantly. You already got value from them — the feed prioritizes showing you things you haven't engaged with yet.

Repost Demotion demotion

Same as above but for posts you've reposted.

Impression Rotation temporary demotion

Posts you've seen recently get progressively pushed down on each refresh. The system tracks when you've actually seen a post (via your Bluesky app) and uses that to build confidence. Posts confirmed seen get stronger demotion. The effect compounds with repeated impressions but recovers over time.

Post-Processing

Diversity Spreader

After scoring, posts from the same author are spaced out so you don't see the same person back-to-back-to-back. Scores are not changed — only the presentation order. If someone has 5 great posts, they'll all appear, just with other people's posts in between.

What You Can Control

You can adjust how the feed works for you at nearyou.atls.city (interests, stats, post inspector) or atls.city/feed-settings (feed settings):

  • City Mode — show posts from your main city only, all your tagged cities, or exact cities with no nearby expansion
  • Post Source — ATlas users only, hashtag posts only, or both
  • Show/Hide Your Posts — whether your own posts appear
  • Media Filter — all posts, media only, or text only
  • Freshness — how aggressively old posts decay (FYP only)
  • Minimum Engagement — filter out posts below a like threshold
  • Alt Text Strictness — how much to penalize posts without image descriptions (FYP only)
  • Mutual Boost — how much to favor posts from people you mutually follow (FYP only)
  • Topic Filters — hide posts related to specific topics (News, Sports, Politics, Crypto)
  • Interest Learning — enable or disable automatic interest detection from your likes
  • Choose Interests — select topics you care about at nearyou.atls.city to immediately boost matching posts
  • Post Inspector — paste any post URL at nearyou.atls.city to see exactly how it scores for you

How This Differs From Other Algorithms

We don't optimize for engagement

Most social media algorithms are designed to maximize the time you spend scrolling. They promote content that triggers reactions — outrage, fear, controversy — because that drives engagement metrics.

The Near You feed optimizes for local relevance. We want to show you what's happening near you, from people near you. Engagement (likes) is one signal among many, not the primary goal.

We actively push down content you've already seen

Most algorithms keep showing you the same viral posts because they drive engagement. We do the opposite: posts you've already liked get demoted. Posts you've seen multiple times get pushed down. We want you to discover new things, not re-engage with the same content.

You can see and adjust the algorithm

This page exists. You're reading it. Most platforms would never publish how their algorithm works because it would reveal that the algorithm serves the platform's interests, not yours.

You can adjust multiple aspects of how the feed works for you. If you don't like how something is weighted, change it. If you want a pure chronological experience with zero algorithmic ranking, use the Near You (chronological) feed instead.

Small accounts can compete

The follower normalization factor (when enabled) means a small local account with genuine engagement can rank alongside or above large accounts. The diversity spreader ensures no single author dominates your feed regardless of their follower count.

What We're Honest About

No algorithm is perfect. Here's what we acknowledge:

  • Popularity bias exists. Posts with more likes score higher. This means already-popular content gets more visibility. The cold start boost and follower normalization help, but don't eliminate this.
  • Affinity can create mild filter bubbles. If you keep liking the same author, you'll see more of them. The diversity spreader and impression rotation mitigate this, but the effect is still there.
  • Hashtag matching has false positives. Someone using #portland might be talking about Portland, Maine. We only match single-city hashtags (multi-city = discarded) but some errors slip through.
  • Location is approximate. We use city-level location, not precise coordinates. Two people in the same city might be 30 miles apart.
  • The algorithm is tunable by the admin. The system operator can adjust all scoring weights. This page describes the general approach, but specific values may change.

Chronological Feed Pipeline

The chronological feed has no scoring. Posts are filtered and sorted by time.

1. Load user citiesRead user's city_mode setting (main/all/exact). Main = primary city + nearby cities within cutoff_radius_km. All = all tagged cities + their nearby cities. Exact = only tagged cities, no expansion.
2. Query posts WHERE author_city_id IN citiesOnly posts with a matching city ID. Posts without a city ID are excluded.
3. Apply source_type filterExclude posts whose source_type doesn't match any enabled source toggle. Posts with hashtag_matched=1 also pass if Hashtag Posts is enabled (OR model).
4. Apply self_post_visibility filterIf set to 'hide', exclude posts where author = requesting user.
5. Apply media_filterall: no filter. media_only: has_media = 1. text_only: has_media = 0 or null.
6. Apply min_engagement filterall: no filter. some: like_count >= 2. popular: like_count >= 5.
7. Sort by indexedAt DESCNewest first. No scoring, no ranking. Pure chronological order.
8. Return pageReturn the first N posts (default 30). Cursor-based pagination for subsequent pages.

FYP Feed Pipeline

The FYP feed scores every candidate post, applies personalization, and returns the highest-scoring posts with variety.

1. Load user citiesSame as chronological: read city_mode, expand to nearby cities within cutoff_radius_km.
2. Load scoring configRead all values from feed_config table (cached in memory, refreshes every 30 seconds).
3. Load user dataIn parallel: served post log, liked posts, reposted posts, follow graph (from ATlas), interaction affinity counts (likes per author), follower counts (from profile cache).
4. Check pagination cacheIf this is a page 2+ request (has cursor) and a cached result exists within pagination_cache_ttl_seconds, return from cache. Otherwise continue to fresh computation.
5. Query candidatesPosts from matching cities within a time window (48 hours or 3x freshness halflife, whichever is larger). Apply same filters as chronological: source_type, self_post_visibility, media_filter, min_engagement.
6. Score each post (19 factors multiplied)Each factor returns a multiplier. All are multiplied together. Disabled factors return 1.0 (neutral). The 19 factors: base_engagement, local_weight, distance_weight, freshness_weight, media_boost, alt_text_weight, self_post_weight, served_demotion, liked_demotion, repost_demotion, follow_boost, affinity_boost, follower_normalization, engagement_velocity_boost, hashtag_post_weight, interest, interest_filter, list_boost, feed_engagement_boost, regional_penalty.
7. Apply impression rotationPosts the user has seen recently get progressively demoted. Each impression multiplies score by rotation_penalty_per_impression (default 0.85). Compounds: 2nd view = 0.85x, 3rd = 0.72x, 4th = 0.61x. Floor at rotation_max_penalty (0.5). Impressions expire after rotation_window_minutes (15). Tracked in memory, no database.
8. Apply score jitterEach post's score is randomly adjusted by +/- score_jitter_factor (default 10%). This shuffles the ranking slightly on each refresh so the feed doesn't feel static.
9. Sort by score DESCHighest scoring posts first.
10. Apply diversity spreaderReorder posts so the same author doesn't appear back-to-back. Enforces a minimum gap of diversity_min_gap (default 3) posts between the same author. Scores are NOT changed — only presentation order. If not enough other authors exist, the gap shrinks gracefully.
11. Cache resultStore the full scored + ordered list in memory for pagination consistency. Expires after pagination_cache_ttl_seconds (default 60).
12. Return page + record impressionsReturn the first N posts. Record which posts were shown to this user for impression rotation tracking. Also log to served_post_log for long-term served_demotion (if enabled).

Ingestion Pipeline

How posts get into the feed system from the Bluesky network.

1. Connect to JetstreamWebSocket connection to Bluesky's Jetstream service. Receives real-time events for posts, likes, and reposts across the entire network.
2. Post event arrivesFor each new post on the network, check if the author is an ATlas user (O(1) lookup against cached set of ~1,800 DIDs, refreshed every 5 minutes).
3a. ATlas user postStore the post with author's primary city ID, media flags, alt text detection, text length. Set hashtag_matched = 0.
3b. Non-ATlas user post (hashtag ingestion)If hashtag_location_ingestion_enabled is true: scan post text and facets for hashtags. Check against cached hashtag mappings. If exactly 1 city matches, store the post with that city ID. If no match, check if the author has earned a city score via rolling hashtag patterns (Author City Discovery) or has a strong local follow graph signal (Follow Graph Discovery). Region/country ATlas users without a city also go through this path.
4. Reply handlingReplies are not stored as feed posts. If a reply's parent post is in our database, increment its reply_count.
5. Like eventIncrement like_count on the post (for all likes). If the liker is an ATlas user, also record the interaction in user_post_interaction (for liked_demotion and affinity_boost). If the post is not in our database, track for community surfacing. Also logs interest signals from the post's content tags, building the user's interest profile over time.
6. Repost eventIncrement repost_count on the post. If the reposter is an ATlas user, also record the interaction.
7. Post deletionRemove the post from the database when a delete event is received.
8. Cursor persistenceSave the Jetstream cursor position every 20 events. On restart, resume from the last saved cursor so no events are missed.
